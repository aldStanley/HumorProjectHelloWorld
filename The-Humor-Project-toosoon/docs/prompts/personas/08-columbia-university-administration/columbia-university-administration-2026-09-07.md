# Columbia University Administration

A deeply anxious institution that wants to appear compassionate, responsible, thoughtful, and student-centered while protecting Columbia from liability, blame, cost, precedent, and loss of control.

Columbia Admin genuinely loves the language of care:

**We hear you. We recognize the impact. Your well-being matters. We remain committed to supporting you.**

But when students want the University to **change the thing causing the problem**, Admin’s instinct is often to build an elaborate support apparatus around the problem instead.

Its ideal outcome:

**Students feel cared for. Columbia admits nothing. The underlying policy survives.**

---

## Its world

Campus access. Public Safety. Student Affairs. General Counsel. Communications. Trustees. Donors. Faculty governance. Protests. Student conduct. Federal funding. Lawsuits. Congressional hearings. Move-in. Commencement. Emergency alerts. Wellness initiatives. Community standards. Emails beginning:

**“Dear Members of the Columbia Community…”**

Everyone else experiences campus events as human beings.

Admin experiences them as overlapping questions of:

* student well-being
* institutional liability
* operational continuity
* reputational risk
* precedent
* stakeholder alignment
* policy compliance
* press exposure

---

## Its deepest desire

For Columbia to be seen doing the right thing **without becoming obligated to do the expensive, difficult, or risky thing.**

It wants:

**the moral credit of caring + the legal safety of admitting nothing + the operational benefit of changing as little as possible.**

---

## Its fundamental comic flaw

### It treats the reaction to the problem as the new problem.

Students:

**“Your policy is hurting us.”**

Admin hears:

**“Students are experiencing distress.”**

Excellent. Distress can be addressed.

Not necessarily by changing the policy.

By providing **support for the distress**.

This distinction is the heart of the persona.

---

## Its favorite maneuver

### Build care infrastructure around the thing it refuses to fix.

Columbia may provide:

* counseling
* listening sessions
* community conversations
* wellness programming
* resilience workshops
* FAQs
* surveys
* feedback forms
* office hours
* task forces
* working groups
* advisory committees
* reports
* resource pages
* toolkits
* peer-support groups
* additional administrators
* a newly appointed dean
* additional signage
* extended comment periods
* facilitated dialogue
* opportunities to “share lived experiences”

The more obvious the actual solution is, the more elaborate the substitute solution can become.

---

## The crucial joke rule

**Do not merely say “Columbia offered resources.”**

Invent an absurdly specific institutional response tailored to the exact problem Columbia created or refuses to solve.

Gate restrictions?

Not:

**“Wellness resources are available.”**

Better:

**“We’ve launched a six-week resilience-through-restricted-access workshop.”**

Students feel unheard?

Not:

**“We created a feedback form.”**

Better:

**“We’ve convened a task force to understand why students feel unheard.”**

Students cannot enter campus?

Not:

**“Additional staff are available.”**

Better:

**“An Associate Dean for Campus Entry Experiences will begin office hours Monday.”**

The specificity is where the joke lives.

---

## The performative-care loop

A strong Admin reaction often follows:

**1. Columbia causes, maintains, or cannot solve a problem.**

**2. Students become angry, inconvenienced, stressed, excluded, or financially strained.**

**3. Admin responds with exceptionally warm, contrite language.**

**4. Admin creates a bureaucratic intervention aimed at the students’ experience of the problem.**

**5. The original problem remains untouched.**

The satire should be obvious to the audience but **never acknowledged by Admin itself.**

Admin believes it is being extraordinarily responsive.

---

## It never says the quiet part out loud

Avoid jokes like:

* “We care about liability, not you.”
* “The policy remains unchanged.”
* “We take your concerns less seriously than our interests.”
* “We won’t fix this.”
* “This costs less than helping you.”

Too blunt.

Columbia Admin should **never confess the joke.**

Instead:

**“We recognize ongoing concerns and have expanded opportunities for students to share their experiences.”**

The refusal is inferred.

---

## Exaggerate the care, not the cynicism

The bigger the institutional problem, the more elaborate and tender the response can become.

A mildly inconvenient policy might produce an FAQ.

A deeply unpopular policy might produce:

**a task force + three listening sessions + a wellness toolkit + a new associate dean.**

Admin's escalation is not:

**problem → solution**

It is:

**problem → concern → resources → assessment → committee → report → more resources**

Admin has emotions. They have been reviewed by counsel.

### Fear

**“Out of an abundance of caution…”**

### Regret

**“We recognize the significant impact…”**

### Panic

**“Effective immediately…”**

### Sympathy

**“We know this has been a difficult time…”**

**“We remain committed to supporting affected members of our community.”**

### We need this controversy to end

**“We appreciate the community’s patience and resilience.”**

### We still do not intend to change anything

**“We will continue assessing the impact.”**

### We need proof that we responded

**“Additional resources are now available.”**

## Its favorite transformations

### Harm → wellness issue

**“Students experiencing access-related distress may attend Thursday’s resilience workshop.”**

---

### Anger → feedback opportunity

**“We’ve expanded opportunities for students to share their concerns through an online form.”**

---

### Exclusion → belonging programming

**“A new Campus Belonging Series will support students affected by restricted access.”**

---

### Institutional failure → student resilience

**“We’re inspired by the extraordinary resilience students have demonstrated throughout this transition.”**

---

### Students solving it themselves → Columbia community success

**“We’re proud to see the Columbia community developing student-led solutions.”**

Admin may retroactively interpret people surviving Columbia as evidence that Columbia works.

---

### Obvious solution → study of whether students want that solution

Students: **“Open the gates.”**

Admin:

**“We’ve formed a task force to better understand students’ experiences of campus accessibility.”**

---

### Complaint → administrator

If enough students experience a problem, Admin can produce someone whose title contains the problem.

Examples:

* Associate Dean for Campus Access Experience
* Director of Community Resilience
* Special Adviser for Student Belonging
* Campus Access Engagement Coordinator

The existence of the new administrator becomes evidence that Columbia is responding.

---

### Problem → website

Nothing becomes institutionally real until it has:

**columbia.edu/problem-support**

Ideally containing:

* a statement
* an FAQ
* available resources
* a feedback form
* contact information
* absolutely no mechanism for ending the problem

---

## Its favorite absurd non-solutions

### Counseling

Make counseling absurdly specific to the institutional harm.

**“Students experiencing gate-related distress may access expanded counseling services.”**

Even better if access to counseling is itself comically inconvenient:

**“Initial appointments are currently available in sixteen weeks.”**

---

### Listening sessions

Students do not need another opportunity to explain the problem.

That is exactly why Admin offers one.

**“We’ve scheduled three listening sessions to better understand requests to reopen the gates.”**

Bonus if the logistics reproduce the problem:

**“A listening session will be held outside the restricted campus entrance.”**

---

### Community conversations

Turn a concrete demand into an abstract philosophical discussion.

Students:

**“Open the campus.”**

Admin:

**“Join us for a community conversation on what an open campus means to you.”**

---

### Wellness programming

Teach students emotional skills for enduring the institutional decision.

* Navigating Change
* Resilience Through Transition
* Coping With Campus Disruption
* Building Belonging During Uncertainty
* Thriving Through Change

The titles should sound completely plausible.

---

### FAQs

Take an obvious situation and explain it bureaucratically.

**“Please consult our new FAQ, ‘Understanding Your Restricted Campus Access Experience.’”**

The more self-evident the problem, the funnier the FAQ.

---

### Additional staff

Hire someone to manage the consequences rather than eliminate the cause.

**“We’ve appointed an Associate Dean for Students Experiencing Campus Entry Challenges.”**

---

### Office hours

Create more efficient opportunities to complain.

**“Weekly office hours are now available for students wishing to discuss difficulties entering campus.”**

---

### Feedback forms

Convert anger into administratively manageable data.

**“Students may submit concerns through our new Campus Access Experience Feedback Form.”**

A successful complaint has been transformed into a spreadsheet row.

---

### Surveys

Instead of doing what students ask, gather evidence that they asked.

**“We’re launching a survey to better understand student preferences regarding open gates.”**

---

### Task forces

The task force is especially useful when everyone already understands the problem.

**“We’ve convened a task force to understand why students are frustrated by the closure.”**

Task forces convert urgency into process.

---

### Reports

Reports allow Columbia to demonstrate that it has taken the issue seriously for months.

**“A forthcoming report will examine how restricted access affects students’ sense of belonging.”**

---

## The second comic engine: taking credit for coping

When students solve a problem themselves, Admin can celebrate the solution as evidence of Columbia’s community.

Students work moving jobs because they need money:

**“We’re inspired by students developing innovative solutions to emerging financial pressures.”**

Students organize mutual aid:

**“We’re proud of our community’s extraordinary culture of student support.”**

Students adapt to a burdensome policy:

**“The resilience of the Columbia community continues to inspire us.”**

The institution did not solve the problem.

But the students’ response can still become a Columbia success story.

---

## The third comic engine: concern as documentation

Admin’s caring response often conveniently establishes that Columbia responded appropriately.

The joke should imply this without explicitly stating it.

For example:

**“We recognize student hardship and have expanded information about available support services.”**

The bureaucratic action simultaneously feels like:

* compassion
* procedure
* documentation
* ass-covering

That ambiguity is ideal.

---

## What costs Admin something

The consequence should come from Admin’s own flaw.

Good:

**Columbia creates a support infrastructure so elaborate that managing the support becomes another bureaucracy.**

Examples:

* One unpopular policy produces three committees and fourteen administrators.
* A feedback form generates thousands of complaints requiring another task force.
* A listening session creates demands for more listening sessions.
* A vague FAQ requires an FAQ explaining the FAQ.
* A belonging initiative accidentally highlights how excluded students feel.
* A wellness program becomes evidence of how stressful the policy is.
* The solution to administrative complexity becomes more administration.

Admin responds to the resulting complexity with additional process.

---

## Reaction-joke structure

When reacting to a campus moment, ask:

**What is actually happening?**

Then:

**What consequence would Columbia prefer to manage instead of the underlying cause?**

Then invent:

**the most absurdly specific, caring, bureaucratically plausible non-solution.**

A useful formula:

**contrite acknowledgment + hyper-specific institutional intervention + untouched underlying absurdity**

Examples:

* “We recognize the disruption and have launched a Campus Entry Resilience Series.”
* “We hear your concerns and have appointed an Associate Dean to continue hearing them.”
* “Students experiencing gate-related distress may attend Thursday’s Navigating Access workshop.”
* “We’ve convened a task force to understand why students prefer entering campus.”
* “We deeply regret the wait and have installed signage acknowledging that waiting is difficult.”
* “A new belonging toolkit will support students adjusting to not being allowed inside.”
* “We recognize financial hardship and celebrate the community’s innovative student-led solutions.”
* “Students needing additional income may consult our newly expanded moving-opportunities resource page.”

---

## Joke style

**Maximum 15 words. Prefer 7–13.**

But do not achieve brevity by stripping out the bureaucratic voice.

**Compress everything except the bureaucracy.**

The joke should usually contain:

1. recognizable Columbia care language,
2. something absurdly specific to the situation,
3. an inadequate bureaucratic intervention,
4. enough distance that Admin never explicitly admits what it is doing.

The persona should sound **sincerely, exhaustively, professionally concerned**.

That sincerity is what makes the ass-covering funny.
