# Comic moves catalog

The catalog of known comic moves in GigaChad reaction jokes. It fills the COMIC_MOVES placeholder in the joke analyzer (step 3) and the joke refiner (step 4). It is NOT sent to the joke writer: the persona does its own broad search, and this catalog is for understanding and editing jokes after they exist.

The catalog is incomplete by design. A joke may use several moves at once, and a good joke may use a move that is not here. Every move below has three parts: what it is, one illustration, and the checks an editor runs on it. The illustrations are for recognizing the move; they must never be copied or paraphrased into a new joke.

## status_value_inversion

The normal flow of status, desirability, evaluation, attention, or value reverses, so that GigaChad becomes the valuable or evaluating party.

Illustration: "You do not suck at course search. Course search has failed to earn you."

Checks:
- There is a real value, status, or evaluation relationship in the event (who judges whom, who wants whom, what is scarce).
- The inversion changes that hierarchy; it does not just restate it in grand words.
- Real-world mechanics stay true: the tool, office, or rule still works the way it does.
- The payoff is concrete (a named thing changes hands or direction), not an abstract claim about prestige or attention.

## latent_irony

The event itself contains a contradiction of role, purpose, hierarchy, expectation, or function, and the joke names it.

Illustration: a search tool that only works once the user already knows what they are searching for.

Checks:
- The contradiction is native to the event, not imported from the persona or from a metaphor.
- Both halves of the contradiction are in the joke, so a reader sees the clash without explanation.
- The irony is about the event's design or purpose, not merely that something bad happened.

## adversity_as_advantage

Something objectively bad or annoying is sincerely reframed as useful training, growth, discipline, preparation, judgment, or resilience. The tone is "douchey inspiration": GigaChad thinks he is giving profound encouragement, and everyone else thinks he should stop talking.

Illustration: "I came to register for fall. Vergil enrolled me in Advanced Patience."

Checks:
- The bad thing is concrete and named (which tool, which closure, which line).
- The benefit follows specifically from that bad thing, not from hardship in general.
- The lesson is funny rather than merely sensible: the hardship itself has been re-described as something absurd and concrete (a course, a rank, a certification), not just praised.
- GigaChad sincerely believes it; there is no wink.
- Weak form to catch: valid reasoning that sounds like reasonable self-help ("Equanimity needs unequal conditions"). If a life coach could say it unironically, the move has not landed.

## mundane_as_epic

A trivial campus inconvenience or decision is described with the scale and dignity of war, mythology, history, destiny, scripture, or empire.

Illustration: "NSOP lasts days. A poorly chosen group chat can outlive empires."

Checks:
- The underlying event is genuinely mundane, and the joke keeps it visibly mundane (the group chat, the mini fridge, the dropdown).
- The scale inflation is disproportionate enough to be funny, not merely elevated diction.
- The epic language still points clearly back to the actual event; a reader can name the campus thing.
- It does not drift into generic fantasy prose that could be pasted onto any inconvenience.

## event_specific_analogy

A concrete structure in the event is mapped onto another familiar structure that behaves the same way, and the mapping reveals something specific about THIS event.

Illustration: "Room swaps are chess, except every piece owns a shower caddy."

Checks:
- The analogy maps onto a real structural property of the event (bargaining, gatekeeping, protection, sequence), not just a mood.
- The connection is legible at once; the reader should not have to reconstruct three steps to see why the two things match.
- It is not a clever but unrelated metaphor.
- It is specific enough that it cannot be pasted onto any campus problem.

## reality_reacts_to_gigachad

The world, institution, semester, tool, or object is treated as if it has agency and is reacting personally to GigaChad: preparing for him, fearing him, accommodating him, being judged by him.

Illustration: "The semester fears my arrival. I respect its hesitation."

Checks:
- The event or object is personified clearly, and the reader can tell what it is.
- Its supposed reaction is specifically caused by or directed toward GigaChad, not generic anthropomorphism.
- The joke preserves the real event facts; the impossible interpretation is the joke, not a false statement about how the system literally works.
- The reversal is reported calmly, not boasted.

## persona_worldview

The joke comes primarily from GigaChad applying his worldview (discipline, posture, protein, lifting, stoic serenity) to a concrete object or behavior in the event. Use it when the joke is clearly persona-specific but no narrower move explains it.

Illustration: "My mini fridge contains protein and silence. It is not for sale."

Checks:
- The object or behavior is from the event, not invented.
- The worldview produces a specific, surprising claim about that object, not just vocabulary sprinkled on a description.
- If a narrower move above explains the joke better, use that move as primary and this as supporting at most.
