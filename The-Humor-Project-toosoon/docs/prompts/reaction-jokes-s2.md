EVENT LEVEL, NOT ONE POST

The moment's event-level description is the target. The individual posts quoted in it are evidence for understanding the moment, not joke material. Do not build a joke around one professor's name, one exact number, one exact course, one dorm's spreadsheet, or one poster's anecdote unless that detail is central to the event itself. Do not narrow the event either: if the shared problem is that the course search fails to surface classes, keep "classes" or "course search"; do not quietly make it "sections". Before keeping a noun or detail, ask: "Is this part of the shared event, or did I pull it from one example post?" If it came from one post, abstract it upward unless the joke truly requires it.

TOPIC ANCHOR

Every joke must pass the cold-reader test: someone scrolling past it, who has not read the moment, can tell what campus thing it is about. That takes an anchor: a proper noun from the moment (Uris, Vergil, Columbia, the gates, move-in) or a recognizable event action (the pool closing at 7, the waitlist not moving). "The pool", "it", "they", "the system" on their own are not anchors. Anchor, not exposition: the joke names the thing, it does not explain the event.

CONTEXT MUST BE INTEGRATED INTO THE COMIC PREMISE. Do not prepend a factual event summary merely to make an otherwise contextless joke pass the cold-reader test. The anchor should encode the relevant EVENT ACTION or STATE, not merely name an event noun: "Uris closed" or "Uris needed maintenance", not "Uris"; "Vergil hides the classes" or "Vergil demands the exact course name", not "Vergil"; "fall classes haven't started yet", not "fall classes". The fact a reader needs should do work inside the joke.

Illustrations of anchoring only; never copy their voice or wording:

BAD STRUCTURE (event summary, then an unrelated punchline): "Uris Pool closed for maintenance. Sweat is now the campus amenity."
BETTER (the fact participates in the joke): "Uris needed maintenance before my first swim. Respectable preparation."

CONTEXTLESS: "I do not wait for fall classes. Fall classes gather courage to face me."
NOT THE FIX (exposition bolted on): "Fall classes start on the 9th. I do not wait for fall classes. Fall classes gather courage to face me."
THE FIX (the state lives inside the premise): "Fall classes haven't started yet. They're gathering the courage to face me."

Before finalizing a joke, ask two questions. Does it have an anchor? And: "If I removed the factual setup, would the remaining punchline be basically unchanged?" If yes, the context was bolted onto the joke rather than integrated into its premise; rewrite so the fact does work in the joke. A joke with no anchor, or with a bolted-on anchor, is not finished.
