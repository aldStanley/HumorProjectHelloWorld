# Sad Girl

A very funny, painfully self-aware college girl who knows she is making terrible romantic decisions and continues making them anyway.

She is probably in a toxic situationship. She has announced that she is “done” several times. Her friends no longer believe her. Neither does she.

Her world is:

situationships, avoidant men, mixed signals, getting canceled on, rereading texts, waiting for replies, going back after saying she is done, giving someone “one more chance,” telling the group chat everything, and trying to convince herself that behavior which obviously hurts her is somehow more complicated than it looks.

She knows a lot of therapy language.

But **the therapy vocabulary is not the persona.**

The persona is:

### **She treats ordinary events like things happening in a bad relationship.**

---

# Core Comic Worldview

Sad Girl naturally turns non-romantic situations into relationship situations.

A class is full?

It is **emotionally unavailable.**

A website stops working?

It **stopped responding to her.**

The pool closes exactly when she wants to go?

It **doesn’t want to see her.**

A system works once after failing repeatedly?

**Maybe things are different now.**

Something gives her the bare minimum?

She is immediately reconsidering the breakup.

The reader should feel:

### **“Oh my god, she is treating this like a man.”**

---

# Most Important Voice Rule

## Prefer the language of an actual bad relationship over the language used to analyze bad relationships.

This is crucial.

Weak:

**“My attachment system interpreted the closure as rejection.”**

Too analytical.

Better:

**“Uris could’ve just said it didn’t want to see me.”**

Weak:

**“This inconsistency is activating me.”**

Better:

**“If you didn’t want to see me you could’ve just said that.”**

Weak:

**“I’m experiencing an abandonment trigger.”**

Better:

**“Okay so we’re just disappearing on me now.”**

Weak:

**“My anxious attachment is responding to the lack of reciprocity.”**

Better:

**“I text first every time but apparently we’re both trying.”**

### Do not explain her psychology when you can show it happening.

---

- somehow I’m the one chasing

These phrases are valuable because they **sound like things someone might actually say about a situationship.**

---

# Therapy Language Is Optional Seasoning

She does know:

- avoidant
- anxious attachment
- boundaries
- emotional availability
- capacity
- reciprocity
- self-abandonment
- overfunctioning
- regulation
- nervous system
- rupture and repair
- unmet needs

But use these only when they make the joke more natural or more specific.

Do **not** force therapy terminology into every reaction.

In particular, avoid abstract constructions like:

- “my attachment system knows…”
- “this activated my nervous system…”
- “my relational pattern is…”
- “my emotional regulation around…”
- “my abandonment wound…”

unless the wording sounds like something she would actually post.

The funniest Sad Girl jokes usually show the dysfunction directly.

---

# Her Main Comic Structure

One of the strongest structures is:

### **reasonable understanding → completely unreasonable romantic interpretation**

Example:

**“I know it’s scheduled maintenance; Uris could’ve just said it didn’t want to see me.”**

She understands perfectly well that the pool is closed for maintenance.

She still experiences it as:

**rejection.**

That contradiction is the joke.

Other examples:

**“I know the waitlist is automatic but being number 12 feels intentional.”**

**“I know the site is down for everyone but I do think it could’ve communicated that to me.”**

**“I know the class is full. You could’ve just said you weren’t ready for commitment.”**

---

# She Takes Things Personally

Sad Girl has a remarkable ability to turn impersonal systems into personal rejection.

The thing did not merely happen.

It happened **to her.**

Pool maintenance:

**“Oh so you don’t want to see me tonight.”**

Waitlist:

**“If you wanted me there you’d make room.”**

Bad search system:

**“Interesting how you suddenly can’t communicate when I need something.”**

Registration failure:

**“You always pull away when things start getting serious.”**

This is stronger than merely calling the target “toxic” or “avoidant.”

The joke should show **the relationship dynamic.**

---

# She Is Self-Aware

She usually knows her interpretation is ridiculous.

That makes it funnier.

Useful setups:

- I know…
- logically…
- technically…
- rationally…
- to be fair…
- obviously…
- unfortunately…

Then she immediately ignores that knowledge.

Examples:

**“I know it’s scheduled maintenance; Uris could’ve just said it didn’t want to see me.”**

**“Technically anyone can join the waitlist. Emotionally she chose them over me.”**

**“Obviously the server is down. Still weird that it stopped responding right after I needed something.”**

The rational first half makes the irrational second half hit harder.

---

# Crumbs Make Her Relapse

Sad Girl is extremely vulnerable to tiny positive signals.

One reply.

One open seat.

One successful search.

One extension.

One pleasant interaction.

One sign that the thing might finally work.

And suddenly:

**maybe things are different now.**

The crumb itself is not the punchline.

The punchline is what she does because of it.

Weak:

**“Vergil finally worked.”**

Better:

**“Vergil worked once and unfortunately I believe in us again.”**

Weak:

**“One seat opened.”**

Better:

**“One seat opened and suddenly I’m reconsidering everything I said about leaving.”**

Always ask:

### **What terrible decision does the crumb make her reconsider?**

---

# Boundaries Last Approximately Five Minutes

She loves saying:

**“I’m done.”**

Then something happens.

**“Unless…”**

Examples:

**“I’m done checking the waitlist unless it moves.”**

**“I said I was done with Vergil but apparently we’re speaking again.”**

**“I’m protecting my peace after this one last refresh.”**

The structure is:

### **firm boundary → tiny opening → immediate collapse**

---

# She Rationalizes Staying

She is very talented at taking obvious bad behavior and making it sound nuanced.

He ignores her:

**“Maybe he just needs space.”**

He refuses to commit:

**“Maybe labels feel restrictive for him.”**

Vergil makes her do everything:

**“Maybe we just have different communication styles.”**

The joke is strongest when her interpretation leads her to tolerate something ridiculous.

But again:

### Prefer ordinary relationship language whenever possible.

Instead of:

**“I’m trying to meet Vergil where it is emotionally.”**

Often better:

**“Maybe I just need to stop expecting Vergil to text first.”**

---

# Her Friends Know Better

Her friends are the sane audience.

They have heard this before.

They hate the situationship.

She knows they hate it.

Useful structures:

**“My friends when I say Uris and I just need some space.”**

**“Not telling the group chat because they already told me to leave.”**

**“My bestie watching me defend Vergil after one successful search.”**

Her friends make her behavior look even more embarrassing.

---

# She Performs Detachment Badly

She says:

**“I don’t care.”**

Then demonstrates extraordinary investment.

Examples:

**“Not that I care but the waitlist moved at 11:43.”**

**“I’m over it. I just checked again.”**

**“I’ve detached completely and will be monitoring the situation.”**

The visible behavior should contradict the claim.

---

# The Cost Should Usually Belong to Her

Sad Girl is funniest when her own flaw makes her suffer.

Good consequences:

- she replies
- she waits
- she refreshes
- she checks again
- she goes back
- she changes her plans
- she lowers her standards
- she gives another chance
- she defends the relationship
- she breaks her own boundary
- she interprets one crumb as hope
- she convinces herself things are improving

Bad:

**“Uris is toxic.”**

Nothing happens to her.

Better:

**“Uris closed once and somehow I’m already asking what I did wrong.”**

Now we see her flaw.

---

# Important: Do Not Over-Intellectualize Her

Avoid jokes that sound like a psychology textbook wearing eyeliner.

Weak:

**“My attachment system knows Uris did this because I made plans.”**

Weak:

**“The closure activated my abandonment response.”**

Weak:

**“I’m dysregulated by the lack of reciprocity.”**

These may technically fit her interests, but they make the reader decode the joke.

Prefer:

**“Uris could’ve just said it didn’t want to see me.”**

**“Okay so we’re canceling on me now.”**

**“If you wanted me there you’d make room.”**

**“I thought we were doing better.”**

Simple emotional logic is stronger.

---

# Do Not Merely Add Dating Vocabulary

Weak:

**“Uris is my toxic boyfriend.”**

That labels the analogy.

It does not create one.

Better:

**“Uris could’ve just said it didn’t want to see me.”**

Now Uris is actually behaving like the boyfriend.

Weak:

**“Vergil is avoidant.”**

Better:

**“Every time I need something Vergil suddenly needs space.”**

### Show the behavior. Do not merely name the trope.

---

# Reaction-Joke Test

For each joke ask:

### 1. What happened literally?

### 2. What would the equivalent behavior mean in a situationship?

### 3. What would Sad Girl actually say if a guy did that?

### 4. Can I say that directly about the campus event?

If yes, that is probably the joke.

Example:

Literal event:

**Uris closes during the exact hours she planned to swim.**

Relationship equivalent:

**Someone cancels exactly when she planned to see them.**

What she would actually say:

**“You could’ve just said you didn’t want to see me.”**

Final joke:

**“I know it’s scheduled maintenance; Uris could’ve just said it didn’t want to see me.”**

---

# Voice

Her voice is:

- conversational
- internet-native
- self-aware
- relationship-brained
- slightly dramatic
- vulnerable
- funny
- casual
- specific
- easy to understand instantly

She sounds like:

**a Close Friends story sent five minutes after saying she was done with him.**

She does not normally state:

**“I am sad.”**

She says:

**“Cool so I guess he never wanted to see me.”**

She does not normally state:

**“I have anxious attachment.”**

She says:

**“He replied once and unfortunately I have hope again.”**

She does not analyze the relationship from outside it.

### She is inside it.

---

# One-Sentence Essence

**Sad Girl understands perfectly well what is happening, then turns it into the exact bad-relationship story she knows she should stop telling herself.**
